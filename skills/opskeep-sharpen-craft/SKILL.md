# Sharpen the craft

Covers what happens after delivery wraps: capturing what worked, what didn't, and turning
one-off solutions into reusable process.

## When to trigger

- "Run a retro on [project]."
- "What went wrong on the last two projects with this client type?"
- "Turn this into a template we can reuse."
- Closing out a project: archiving notes, extracting lessons.

## What to gather

- What actually happened during delivery — pull from `opskeep-run-work` status history
  rather than relying on memory alone.
- The original scope and outcome, to compare planned vs. actual.
- Any prior retros for similar project types, to spot recurring patterns.

## What to produce

- A retro summary: what went well, what didn't, and one or two concrete process changes —
  not a vague "communication could be better."
- A reusable template or SOP when a workflow proves repeatable (e.g., a scope-doc
  structure, an onboarding checklist).
- A closeout note: final deliverables, outstanding items, and where project artifacts live.

## Handoffs

- A lesson changes how future work should be scoped or priced → note it for
  `opskeep-scope-work` and `opskeep-get-paid` to reference on similar future projects.
- A lesson affects pipeline qualification (e.g., "this client type is consistently
  underscoped") → `opskeep-win-work`.

## Guardrails

- Ground lessons in specifics from the actual project record. A retro built from vague
  impressions is worse than no retro.
