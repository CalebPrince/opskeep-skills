# Scope the work

Covers the gap between "they want to hire us" and "we know exactly what we're building and
for how much."

## When to trigger

- "Turn these discovery notes into a scope doc."
- "Draft a proposal for [project]."
- "What are we missing before we can quote this?"
- Onboarding a newly-signed client: contracts, kickoff info, access requests.

## What to gather

- Raw discovery input: call notes, emails, a messy brain dump — whatever exists.
- Any rate card, package, or prior similar-project scope to anchor pricing and scope size.
- Constraints the client has stated (deadline, budget ceiling, must-haves).

## What to produce

- A structured scope: deliverables, timeline, exclusions, assumptions, and acceptance
  criteria — specific enough that "done" isn't a debate later.
- A proposal draft when asked, built from the scope rather than duplicating the work.
- An onboarding checklist for a newly-signed client: access needed, kickoff date, first
  deliverable, and who owns each item.

## Handoffs

- Signed scope, ready to start → `opskeep-run-work` for delivery coordination.
- Scope includes pricing/payment terms → note them for `opskeep-get-paid` so the first
  invoice matches what was actually agreed.

## Guardrails

- If discovery notes are incomplete (no budget, no timeline, no clear deliverable), say so
  explicitly rather than inventing plausible-sounding numbers.
